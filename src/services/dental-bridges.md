You can lose teeth in many different ways, including dental work, or through accidents and other mishaps. In the past, the loss of a tooth was simply something most people had to learn to accept. Today, there are many alternatives to ensure that a missing tooth doesn't have to become an embarrassment. One technique called a [Dental Bridge in Alexandria VA](/) is a unique solution to the problem.

A dental bridge is designed to address a particular kind of missing tooth problem for dental patients in Alexandria VA. If there is a "space" in your mouth where you have a tooth, followed by a missing tooth and then another tooth, a dental bridge is a perfect solution. It is important not just for the sake of appearances, but even for your overall dental health that such a sizable gap is not allowed to persist.

Missing a tooth in a row of teeth can interfere with both your ability to speak and eat. Without an equal amount of pressure from all sides, the teeth where a gap is present may eventually become crooked with nothing to hold them securely in place. The unequal distribution of forces in your mouth when you eat, speak, and bite down can also affect your other teeth and even, potentially the shape of your face and lips.

## How Dental Bridges In Alexandria VA Work

Where the dental bridge comes in is by filling this gap between the teeth. If you decide to have a dental bridge installed, then a dentist will smooth out the teeth adjacent to the empty space. He will flatten them and turn them into a base or "crown," that is ready to receive the dental bridge.

The dental bridge itself will look like a complete row of three teeth. But only the middle tooth is "complete" from top to bottom, designed to fill out space in your mouth. The other two teeth have been personalized to your mouth to fit the crown that the dentist made. They will have a snug, tight fit that will be just as secure as your normal teeth.

## Types of Dental Bridges

There is a variety of different dental bridge techniques designed to address different needs:

### Traditional Dental Bridge
The traditional dental bridge works as described above, and is usually intended for teeth on the side. This is the most common type of bridge, where crowns are placed on the teeth adjacent to the gap, and a false tooth (pontic) fills the space.

### Maryland Bonded Bridge
For front teeth, the Maryland bonded dental bridge is the recommended technique. This type uses a metal or porcelain framework that bonds to the back of adjacent teeth, requiring minimal tooth preparation.

### Cantilever Bridge
If there's only room for one [crown](/dental-crowns-faqs/) or anchor, a cantilever dental bridge may be used if space is at a premium. This bridge is anchored on only one side of the missing tooth.

## Bridge Materials

Dental bridges are made in a variety of materials including:

- **Porcelain** - Provides the most natural appearance and can be color-matched to your existing teeth
- **Gold** - Extremely durable and long-lasting, often used for back teeth
- **Other alloys** - Various metal combinations that offer strength and durability

They can be colored to match the color of your own teeth as closely as possible. Once affixed, they generally don't require any more extra care of your mouth than normal brushing or flossing which is a part of good dental hygiene.

## Caring for Your Dental Bridge

Proper care will help your bridge last for many years:

- **Daily Cleaning**: Brush twice daily and floss regularly, paying special attention to the area around the bridge
- **Special Flossing**: Use floss threaders or water flossers to clean under the bridge
- **Regular Checkups**: Visit Dr. Mazhari for routine examinations and cleanings
- **Avoid Hard Foods**: Be cautious with very hard or sticky foods that could damage the bridge
- **Don't Use Teeth as Tools**: Avoid using your teeth to open packages or bottles

## Bridge vs. Other Options

### Dental Bridges vs. Dental Implants
While both replace missing teeth, bridges use adjacent teeth for support, while implants are independent. Bridges are often more suitable when adjacent teeth already need crowns.

### Bridges vs. Partial Dentures
Bridges are fixed in place and feel more like natural teeth, while partial dentures are removable and may feel less secure.

## The Bridge Procedure

Getting a dental bridge typically involves:

1. **Initial Consultation**: Evaluation and treatment planning
2. **Tooth Preparation**: Adjacent teeth are prepared to receive crowns
3. **Impressions**: Detailed molds are taken for custom fabrication
4. **Temporary Bridge**: A temporary bridge protects prepared teeth
5. **Final Placement**: The permanent bridge is fitted and adjusted
6. **Follow-up**: Ensuring proper fit and comfort

## Who is a Good Candidate?

You may be a good candidate for a dental bridge if you:

- Have one or more missing teeth
- Have healthy teeth adjacent to the gap
- Have good oral hygiene habits
- Have sufficient bone support
